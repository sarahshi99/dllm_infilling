\section{Introduction}
Diffusion language models require a masked canvas before denoising, whereas real infilling does not reveal the missing length. A failure after length expansion is therefore ambiguous: the original canvas may have been insufficient, or the current generator, seed, decoding schedule, budget, length definition, or evaluator may still prevent recovery.

We organize the evidence around this distinction rather than presenting repeated threshold tuning as a new method. Our current contributions are: (i) a diagnostic decomposition of canvas inadequacy, action-specific non-recovery, and selection harm; (ii) audits across LLaDA, Dream-Coder, and official HumanEval MultiLine/RandomSpan variants while preserving a sealed controller test; and (iii) a transparent negative account of why oracle headroom has not yet become a safe deployable selector. These contributions remain provisional until protocol-matched official baselines, base-task-group statistics, and non-HumanEval evaluation are complete.
