\begin{abstract}
Unknown-length diffusion code infilling requires choosing both content and generation space. We separate two questions that are often conflated: whether the allocated canvas is sufficient and whether the current decoding or rescue action can generate a correct infill once space is available. Across H200 LLaDA diagnostics, a Dream-Coder audit, and HumanEval MultiLine/RandomSpan stress data, oracle-sufficient canvas recovers many failures but can also harm previously correct outputs; deployable selectors do not yet harvest this headroom reliably. A full 6707-span audit covers only 148 independent HumanEval base tasks, so we treat row totals as descriptive and require cluster-aware inference. An inference-visible AST/def-use proxy shows promising selection gains but fails its preregistered superiority gate. The present evidence therefore supports a bounded empirical diagnosis, not a SOTA method claim. Protocol-matched official baselines, cluster-aware statistics, a non-HumanEval benchmark, and a fresh validated method remain required.
\end{abstract}
